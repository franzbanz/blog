Why hydrogen?

- zero emission: no direct co2 emissions and has the potential to reduce nox and water vapor emissions
- versatility
- declining costs

combination of hydeogn and oxygen reacts in the fuel cell to produce electricity - needs to be cooled\
large portion of engine is taken up by cooling system\

### technical challenges of storage

- hydrogen molecule is very small (80pm in largest direction)
- material compatibility
  - permeation
  - hydrogen embrittlement
- temperature compatibility
  - thermal expansion
  - thermal protection
- low density
  - hydrogen three times lighter per unit energy
- Hydrogen tank does not save H2 at state that is needed by engine - conditioning needed
  - to transfer fuel from tank state to engine state, 90kW of thermal energy are needed - heat loss of fuel cell can be used for this
  - per 1MW of themal energy: 20 g/s of H2 mass flow possible
- Tank pressure has to be kept constant with boiler
- In case of direct combustion: very high pressure is needed - can be achieved with pump
  - pumps require a certain level of net positive suction pressure (NPSP) at the inlet to avoid cavitation
  - NPSP drops to zero when tank is mixed (e.g. sloshing during a maneuver)

solid / liquid matter may show non expected behaviour e.g.

- thermal conductivity of copper (goes way up at 25K)
- ortho-para-transition of H2 (quantum effect)
- ductile-brittle fracture mechanics of austenitic steel at specific temperatures
- cryo-pumping
- change of emissivity
- change of thermophysical material properties

don't assume everything stays the same at different (especially lower) temperatures!
