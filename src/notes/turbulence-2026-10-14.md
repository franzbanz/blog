## Generation of turbulent motion

- related to the non-linear character of the navier stokes (momentum) equations
- at high reynolds numbers, any random perurbation grows and produces a chaotic random motion: flow instability and non-linearity
- For turbulence, three things are needed:
  - Re much larger than 1: inertia dominates viscous damping
  - Disturbance to trigger non-linear convective term
  - Transition: instability - non-linear growth - turbulence

## The Energy Cascade

**\\ skizze folie 25**\
energy transfer to smaller scales at rate $\varepsilon$\
viscosity is determining the size of the smallest structures $\ell$ inside the turbulence\
direct numerical simulation: no model: cover the full lifespan of the eddies (grid needs to be smaller than smallest structure) - very very expensive computationally

**slide 26**: large eddies on the left side, small eddies on the right side\

### Large and Small Scales

|large scales ($\ell \propto L$)| small scales ($\ell \propto \eta$|
|--|--|
|generated by the meaen flow|generated by larger eddies|
|depend on geometry and boundary conditions|universal, independent of the geometry|
|**anisotropic** and inhomogeneous, clear structure|statistically isotropic and homogeneous|
|carry most of the kinetic energy, long-lived|little energy, short-lived, dissipative|
|hard to model - no universal model|easier to model - universal models plausible|

- the dissipation rate is set by the large scales, it does not depend on the viscosity
- viscosity only determines the scale at which the energy is finally dissipated
- lower viscosity means smaller dissipative eddies, same dissipation rate
